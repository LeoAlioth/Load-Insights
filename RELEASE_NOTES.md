# Release Notes

## 0.1.0

### New Features

- **Consumption forecast from the Energy dashboard**: Load Insights reads the sources and individual devices you have already set up in Home Assistant's Energy dashboard - grid import/export, PV, battery, metered devices - and the recorder's hourly statistics for them, and publishes a consumption forecast: expected power over the coming hour, today's and tomorrow's kWh, and a seven-day hourly series in the same shape the PV forecast integrations use, so one dashboard card can plot both. A fourth sensor forecasts the **unmetered remainder** - consumption minus every individually metered device - which is what nothing else on the site measures. Recomputed every hour from twelve weeks of history; nothing to configure beyond a name. Public holidays are treated as Sundays, in the history the profile learns from and in the week it forecasts, wherever Home Assistant's Workday integration is set up (its `holidays` library is what supplies them). A device added to the dashboard recently does not shorten the remainder's history: before its first statistic it counts as zero, since its energy was unmetered - and therefore part of the remainder - until then; the sensor's `remainder_complete_since` attribute says from when every device is covered.
- **A forecast per metered device**: every device the Energy dashboard lists gets its own forecast sensor in the same shape, ready to use. A device added to the dashboard later appears after a reload.
- **Actual beside forecast**: every forecast sensor also carries `history`, the last 48 hours as they actually happened, in the same shape as `detailedForecast` - so one entity feeds both halves of an actual-vs-forecast chart (a Plotly Graph Card example is in the README).
- **Download diagnostics**: one file with every forecast's fit, what the Energy dashboard said, the scoring ledger and load detection's whole library - the thing to attach to a question about a number, instead of a screenshot of each attribute.
- **It says when something is half-done**: a weather entity without a temperature sensor (or the reverse), a PV site with no forecast linked, a metered device that publishes only energy - each raised as a repair notification while it is true, and withdrawn when it is fixed. None of them is an error, which is exactly why they used to go unnoticed.
- **Two services**: `refresh` recomputes now instead of on the hour, for checking whether a change took effect; `reset_detection` forgets the signature library and starts the backfill again.
- **Icons, and the site device lands in the grid meter's area.**
- **One sensor per thing forecast, and a metered device's forecast lives on that device**: the state is the prediction for the coming hour and the attributes carry the rest, so daily totals, import and export figures and the scores are attributes rather than entities of their own - a template sensor makes one wherever you want it. A device's forecast is attached to the Home Assistant device behind its Energy dashboard entry, so the boiler's forecast appears on the boiler; only loads found by profiling the meter, which have no device of their own, get a new one.
- **What the meter will do, not just what the house needs**: a grid forecast - consumption less the PV your Energy dashboard's own forecast integrations predict, with the battery simulated in between - published as the expected meter power for the coming hour, tomorrow's import and export in kWh, and the pack's state of charge hour by hour. The battery model follows the house rather than any charge policy, and says so; without an SOC or a capacity on the dashboard it is skipped and the net reported before the battery.
- **Base load**: what the site draws with nothing switched on, per phase and per meter - the idle floor load detection tracks anyway. Worth watching for creep.
- **Load detection, first stage**: give Load Insights the meter's raw per-phase readings (Configure -> Load detection) and it watches them at the meter's own cadence for loads switching on and off - as sessions with levels, merged across phases, matched into signatures it describes in words: "6.1 kW on A+C, ~80 s, every 3 min, seen 258 times". A load is a STEP UP paired with the step down that undoes it, so loads may overlap freely - the kettle inside the fridge's hour closes on its own - and a slow drift, whether the sun coming up on a grid meter or an element tapering, is never mistaken for one. Where the Energy dashboard has an array, a cloud is recognised as the sun rather than filed as a load switching on. Meters below the main one need no setting up: every device on your Energy dashboard is used automatically, so a load that the main meter and a device's own meter both see is placed on that device - and where the dashboard says one device sits inside another, the innermost one wins. Two new sensors, *Detected loads* and *Unknown load power*. What no device meter accounts for you name once, on its own page - each named load then gets a running and a power sensor, and two given the same name are one device on different settings, which the page suggests when it sees them. Give the meter its per-phase voltage and current (or a power factor sensor) and each load's OWN power factor follows, from how far the watts and the reactive power moved when it switched: with that, every signature is offered a guess at what KIND of thing it is - a heating element, a motor, electronics, an inverter-driven appliance, a programme, a car charging - with the readings the guess rests on, two families named where both fit, and never a claim, since shape cannot tell a hair dryer from a fan heater. Each also says where it is by exclusion, "in Hiša, outside Blaževa Soba", or the phase it is on where no meter saw it, and carries three separate confidences: whether it is a real repeating load, what kind it is, and where it lives. Loads seen only once are kept but not offered for naming. Using what is learned in the forecast follows.
- **A device's own state sharpens its next hours**: give a device its state sensor under Configure - the boiler its tank temperature, the pump its level - and its current reading adjusts that device's forecast for the coming six hours, with the spread narrowing where the state explains what happens. Learned from the sensor's recorded past against the device's consumption; sign and size come from the data, and a sensor that turns out not to matter is ignored.
- **Any sensor as an explanatory input**: attach a tariff block, an electricity price, irradiance, an occupancy sensor - anything - and Load Insights works out from its recorded history whether it explains consumption, the same way it does for a calendar, and ignores it when it does not. A sensor with few values is read as it is; a numeric one is cut into bands. Inputs that are a pure function of weekday and hour, tariffs included, will not engage: the weekly profile already knows them.
- **Calendars, without saying what they mean**: link any calendars under Configure and the forecast learns from their past events what each does to consumption - an absence calendar shows up as the house dropping to its base load by day, a bookings calendar as busier evenings - and applies that to the week ahead where their events fall. Event titles are learned on top, only for how they differ from the calendar's average, so one shared calendar with "away" and "guests" entries yields both roles. A calendar that turns out to mean nothing for consumption is ignored, and the `calendars` attribute on every forecast sensor shows what was learned.
- **The forecast scores itself**: what it predicted for each hour a day and a week before is remembered and compared with what happened. Six sensors give the trailing week's mean error and bias for the site and for the unmetered remainder, the hour-ahead error, and yesterday's day-ahead error in kWh; attributes carry the per-lead table and how often the actual fell inside the forecast's spread. Per-device scores are an attribute on each device forecast sensor. The first figures appear after a day, the day-ahead ones after two.
- **The forecast responds to the weather**: link a weather entity (pre-filled when there is exactly one) and an outdoor temperature sensor, at setup or later under Configure, and every forecast learns how much cold and heat add to consumption - from the sensor's recorded past, applied to the weather entity's coming week. It only takes effect where it actually explains consumption; the boiler will show a response, the car charger will not, and each sensor's `temperature_response` attribute shows which.
- **A spread on every forecast hour**: `detailedForecast` rows carry `kwh_p10` and `kwh_p90` beside `kwh` - the weighted 10th and 90th percentiles of that hour-of-week's history, in the PV forecast integrations' naming. An hour where the car charges on some weeks shows the range, not an average that never happens. The forecast attributes are also no longer written to the recorder database on every refresh; they stay live and on cards.
